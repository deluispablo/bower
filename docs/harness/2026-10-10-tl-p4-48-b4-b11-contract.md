# Technical-lead decision TL-P4-48: the B4 (About me) and B11 (views) contracts

These are the rulings on the B4 preparation (p4-b4-prep b36c9160, 9 questions) and the B11 preparation (p4-b11-prep 41816e9c, 8 questions). Both drafts are accepted as the base, with their corpus cases and entry splits. Both follow TL-P4-39 and its addenda, TL-P4-41 to TL-P4-47, and the owner's principle of designing for normal use: one general mechanism, and nothing built for edits outside Bower.

## B4: About me

- **Q1. The note's shape.**
  - About me stays one note. The person's part on top is never touched.
  - Bower's part is one section at the end, `## What Bower has learned`, with three Bower regions: `about-you`, `about-working-on` and `about-preferences`, labelled About you, What you're working on and How you like things.
  - When the note has no such section, Bower appends it once at the end as a byte append (the same kind of edit as C05's), keeping every earlier byte. It never marks the whole file as Bower's.
  - The additive grammar is this: a person's note may end with marked Bower regions. In v1 only About me does.
  - A missing About me is created from the existing folder template. Two About me notes, or damaged marks, mean no write that run.
- **Q2. Observations.** As recommended:
  - versioned JSONL events in `.bower/observations.jsonl`, with script-issued ids, a section, a date, text of at most 200 characters, the source id and hash, and a typed origin resolved only from admitted capture;
  - a document may state facts about its explicit subject (a CV about the person), but never preferences, sensitive facts, rules or schema;
  - a torn or unreadable log freezes memory writes for that run, logged as a BowerError.
- **Q3. Forget and identity: simplified.**
  - **The person's own text in About me is not a source of observations.** It is read directly as trusted context (position 4), so it needs no copies, no tombstones and no deletion tracking: editing it is enough. This drops the own-text-deletion mechanism and case 4.
  - Forget comes from the person's words in a pile and targets shown fact ids. Tombstones are written by fact and by source before the view changes.
  - A source binned in Bower (a settled Bin) tombstones the facts bound only to it.
  - Saying "remember it again" later is a new observation from the person's words; a tombstone is never erased.
- **Q4. Budgets.** About you 400 words, What you're working on 400, How you like things 200, as recommended. The gate is over budget plus at least 5 new live observations since that section's last consolidation. Forget and Undo rebuild without the gate.
- **Q5. The archive.** `.bower/archive/about-me/<section>/<date>-<op id>.md`, write-once, holding the exact pre-consolidation section. There is one About me, so the path needs no file id. The log is the only rebuild source; an archive never grants authority.
- **Q6. Order and recovery.** As recommended: observation or tombstone applied, archive confirmed, one About me edit, the consolidation frontier recorded, then maintain and the barrier. The consolidation text is frozen in the persisted manifest, with no fresh model call on replay. `.bower/observations.jsonl` and the archive path join the existing runner-write allowlist explicitly, with no second effect lifecycle. B4's costs share the 300 calls and the 20% reserve.
- **Q7. Undo.** Undoing a run invalidates its observations and rebuilds from the live evidence. Undoing a Forget does not bring the fact back; remembering it again takes new words from the person.
- **Q8. Context.** At most 4,000 characters of Bower's section at position 4, with an honest cut mark. The person's part is typed person input. An unchanged run writes nothing.
- **Q9. What the person sees.** Only the About me note itself and the existing created/updated rows; that is note content and needs no new OK. An unclear Forget asks through Q1's existing question piece: the model words the question, and the options are the candidate facts bound by script, as one more stored option kind, `forget-fact`, under Q1's contract. That adds no new UI. Any new button, guide or editor is out of scope.

## B11: views

- **Q1. The language.** The closed JSON AST is accepted with its limits: 4 KiB, 32 nodes, depth 8, 4 groups, 8 measures or outputs, 10 views per home and a top 20. There are no functions beyond the listed ones. Writes are strict and reads are lenient.
- **Q2. Semantics.** As recommended: missing or invalid values are excluded and counted, money is partitioned by currency, dates are UTC date-only, arithmetic must be finite, the `if` is typed, and computed values never feed computed values, so cycles cannot happen. Units are labels bound to the request only, with no inferred units and no exchange rates.
- **Q3. Storage and output.** As recommended. An aggregate view is declared as `bower_views` in its Bower MOC's front matter, and its results go in the existing `moc-figures` region. A formula is declared in the type row's `views`, and its computed properties are written only on Bower notes. A person's MOC and notes get nothing, and sources stay immutable.
- **Q4. Consent.** As recommended. An explicit request installs exactly what it asked for. Anything else is a B5 proposal (at most 2 per run, at most 5 open, declined payloads never return). On a yes, the payload is revalidated against the current row; if it no longer fits, nothing is installed and a new proposal is made.
- **Q5. Provenance.** Accepted: result records go in maintain's persisted declaration, holding the declaration hash, evaluator version, input ids and versions, the as-of date, the values and the unavailable counts. They are evidence, never authority.
- **Q6. Writes: simplified.** Computed values are written by maintain after the settled effects, as maintain's own single edit of each target, with a fresh modifiedTime. "One edit per id" holds per stage: a target already edited by the run's primary work gets one more maintain edit, which needs no merging machinery. Cycles cannot occur (Q2).
- **Q7. Dates and columns.** The as-of date is the run's admission date, kept on a retry. Each new run recomputes day counts as part of maintain, with no extra discovery read. One requested column replaces the 4th (approved). **Two or more columns in one request** go beyond the approved Table behaviour: the designer specs it and the owner OKs it before the B11 entry that needs it. Until then, the first requested column is installed and the report says the rest was not done, using today's done line plus the words of a not-done row the designer approves.
- **Q8. Bounds.** At most 200 notes per view, within the shared maintenance budget, with membership checked before reading. Over the bound, before B12 lands, the figures region says honestly "Not computed: more than 200 notes." That is note content, never a partial total. After B12, the large-request yes applies. A known input that cannot be read stops that view, while a vanished file follows B1's drop rule.

## Order

B4 follows B5a and B6a, which share contracts. Its entries are B4a (shape and contracts, escalated), B4b (observations, Forget and Bin) and B4c (consolidation, archive and recovery). B11 follows B5b: B11a (language and validation), B11b (MOC aggregates) and B11c (formulas and dates). Each entry follows TL-P4-29. Cases are accepted, except B4 case 4, which is dropped by Q3.
