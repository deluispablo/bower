## Research first

The standard solution is one frame-time motion owner with completion-based transitions.
[MDN requestAnimationFrame](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Window/requestAnimationFrame)
describes progressing animation with frame timestamps rather than counting frames.
Lynch and Park, *Modern Robotics*, §9.2.2
([book](https://hades.mech.northwestern.edu/images/2/25/MR-v2.pdf)) is the trapezoidal-profile
reference already documented in `engine.ts`. The existing time-based head profile, speed and
acceleration caps, and snap ownership through the stationary landing frame are retained.
These URLs were not fetched: the session has no network.

`app/src/components/bird.tsx:390–429` under `/Users/deluisp/Projects/bower`:

The rebuilt engine's act channel, gaze, pose script and injected frame clock are the homes reused here.
The motion mount remembers completion and adjusts changed inputs during render, following

Scoped ESLint verifies this without disabling the state-in-effect rule.

## What changed

- `react.ts` is the one screen port. Its signed `REACTIONS` strings and kind type are unchanged.
  It reads the active engine and that engine's gaze frame from presence, measures an element's
  centre in frame pixels, and calls only `play(kind, point)`. No screen drives rig parts.
- `BirdEngine.play` gives each kind one composite act through `makeAct`, on `S.doing`.
  Reactions stay outside the mind's weighted draw. They own the head and pause the pose script
  through `poseActed`; gaze still moves the iris. A new reaction cuts the current reaction.
  The existing head profile handles the target changes and returns.
- Wake, nudge, bin, error and offline share `makeStartle`, an arrival-based step. Its tuft rises
  independently in 0.15 s; the head remains a snap through its stationary arrival; the full
  0.6 s hold starts after both arrive. No independent startle animation remains.
- `S.pending` replaces `S.tap`. It holds the latest kind, target and cancellation state during
  layer flight/carry, then plays on landing ahead of the next script action. Ending a pending
  reaction prevents replay. A new reaction can cut an already reacting perched carry without
  discarding its chip. Tap's hop, chirp and tail kick remain observable through the real port.
- Point-tidy and drag-over ease in, hold, and ease out on `end()` or the 10 s engine-time cap.
  Their cap starts at input time. Part timelines begin from the drawn part values.
- A mind-asleep bird wakes before reacting and resets input recency and the 60 s clock.
  An explicit sleep pose ignores ordinary reactions; error/offline wake it, hold the alarm
  while the message remains, and return to sleep on `end()`.
- `useBirdEngine` owns creation and release. Turning moves off calls the port and keeps the
  engine, handle and animated frame through the 0.6 s settle. The engine reports completion
  after applying the last frame, then the mount releases it and shows the CSS still frame.
  A layer keeps that same engine. Reduced motion or loss of the turn releases immediately.
  On enabling moves the engine exists before the wake-and-shake call, including initially
  still mounts. A pose change during settlement updates the same settle's target.
- Settings alone settles to head 8° and eye 0.08, then holds those values with static CSS styles
  on the existing parts. Other setting-off frames keep eyes open. The sleep pose's setting
  transitions use mount context on this same port, without permitting ordinary sleep reactions.
  The Settings identity matches the app's `/settings` route.
- BirdLab has a reactions row with the signed descriptions, action targets and an End reaction
  button. Its existing layer is the active bird. There are no screen/report/online/prefs changes;
  those calls remain d-v2-24b's work.

Second-way check: no second engine, queue, controller, pose script, head profile or startle path.
The port's home is `src/bird/react.ts`; dispatch and composite timelines belong to `engine.ts`;
engine lifetime belongs to `useBirdEngine`; presence owns the active handle and its frame.
