# Technical-lead decision TL-P4-09: a scenario battery after P4

**Owner request, 2026-10-08.** When P4 ends, before the cutover is taken as settled, Bower gets a large battery of realistic use cases: what a person would really do with a second brain, beyond the per-capability corpus cases (C04–C15). The lead drafts the scenarios here, and the owner reviews them when P4 closes. Everything is made up: names, companies, places and documents.

**Shape: stories, not single cases.** Each scenario is a sequence of 3–10 inboxes over simulated weeks, with a tidy-up after each. After every run it checks the state of the whole folder, not just that run's effects:
- notes and their Current state and Timeline;
- hubs and links;
- questions and the report.

What makes a second brain good shows only over time: updates, contradictions, an answer that uses material from weeks earlier, a project that ends.

**Scenario families (draft):**
1. **Job hunt.** Two offers arrive weeks apart, plus an interview invitation, a rejection and three CV versions. The person asks "compare the offers", and a late counter-offer changes the salary.
2. **Moving house.** Listings and viewing photos, then a signed lease, the deposit receipt, utility contracts and change-of-address letters. The rent rises at renewal, and the project ends and is archived.
3. **Money.** A payslip every month and bills, receipts and subscriptions. A price-rise letter updates the subscriptions note, tax documents arrive, and a rule says "receipts by month". A bank letter carries an account number that must never be written into a note.
4. **Health.** Appointment letters, a prescription, test results and an insurance claim and its answer. The person asks "when is my next check-up?". Sensitive identifiers stay out of notes.
5. **Travel.** Bookings, an itinerary, a schedule change that contradicts the booking, a cancellation and a refund, and visa paperwork. Web lookup is allowed and then denied.
6. **Learning and reading.** Web clippings, articles, book notes and a course certificate. Topic notes grow and connect across months, and the person asks a question answered from three sources.
7. **Home and car.** Warranties, manuals, two repair quotes to compare, a service reminder, and an insurance renewal whose premium disagrees with last year's.
8. **Paperwork.** Letters from the town hall, a form in another language (notes in English), a deadline, scans with names that say nothing, and an unreadable file.
9. **A project with an end.** A wedding or event plan, or a renovation: tasks, quotes, deadlines, a request for "a packing list" or "a budget table", then completion and archive.
10. **People and family.** A school newsletter, a children's activity calendar and a contact who changes address.

**Behaviours each family must exercise at least once:**
- duplicates and near-duplicate names;
- an update over time, and a contradiction with both sources;
- a question, a request for a document, a rule, and the person's text edited between runs;
- an ask about an ambiguous item, and the answer to it (TL-P4-08, `ask-answer-loop`);
- undo;
- a large inbox (20+ things), an empty inbox, a long PDF, a foreign-language document;
- web allowed and denied.

**Scoring.**
- **Structural checks** are automatic: placement, notes made, links that resolve, numbers grounded, nothing invented, nothing personal.
- **An independent reviewer** judges usefulness against a short rubric per run.
- **Speed and tokens** are recorded per run.
- **Comparison:** the same stories go through the owner's local method as a reference reading. They are judged by rubric, never by running on the owner's vault.

**Owner decided, 2026-10-08:** an answer starts a small tidy-up at once (TL-P4-08 addendum 8).
