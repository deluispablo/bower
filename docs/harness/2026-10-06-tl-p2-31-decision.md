# Technical-lead decision TL-P2-2026-10-06-31: Remove bins a pile's declaration copies

Lead: Claude `Planificador`. This answers the ratification request from `loose-pile-declaration`. It amends TL-P2-03 and TL-P2-06, which say that only a disposition retires a declaration.

## Ruling: option (ii), one rule for every pile

When the person removes a pile, Bower sends to the Bin, recoverably and never hard-deleted:

- the pile's folder, if it has one;
- **every copy of its declaration**.

Undo restores both. A declaration copy found in the Bin is ignored, and its skip is logged once. This applies to loose files and folder piles alike.

**Why.**

- **The person's explicit Remove is the retirement.** It is the same intent as a person-bin disposition. The difference is that it acts on the records themselves, so it needs no Bin evidence for a folder that may never have existed, as with a loose file that never arrived.
- **It is the usual soft-delete pattern.** Deleting an item moves it and its own records to the trash together, and restoring brings them back together ([Google Drive: trash and restore](https://support.google.com/drive/answer/2375102)).
- **One rule, not two.** No pile reappears after Remove on any device.

Disposition-based retirement for runs stays as it is. This adds the person's Remove as a second, explicit way to end a declaration. TL-P2-30 still governs folders moved or deleted outside Bower, and this ruling does not revive `declared-pile-folder-lost`.
