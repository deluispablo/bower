# Technical-lead decision TL-P2-2026-10-06-28: simplest proven approach, starting with the back button

## Owner rule (2026-10-06)

When a problem turns complex, especially a rare case like several sessions, the browser's back button or concurrency, take the simplest approach that works:

- **Look first** at how successful apps and other repositories already solve it.
- **Copy that** rather than designing our own mechanism.
- **Do not chase perfection** in rare cases. Limiting what the person can do, or simply reloading, is acceptable when it is honest and loses no data.

Lead and coordinator apply this before opening a root-cause investigation or a new mechanism. A real data-loss defect still gets its root cause and a test.

## The back button (`single-active-session` bfcache rejoin)

A page restored from the browser's back-forward cache comes back frozen, with stale state: lease, status, queue. The common, documented pattern is to treat that restoration as a reload ([web.dev on bfcache](https://web.dev/articles/bfcache)). So:

- **Reload on restore.** On `pageshow` with `event.persisted === true`, the app calls `location.reload()`. A full reload runs the normal start: it checks the lease, reads fresh data and resumes the queue from the device.
- **Nothing is lost.** Outbox lines and uploads with a copy on the device survive a reload by design. Uploads held only in memory are already covered by the N1 notice ("keep Bower open").
- **Stop the root-cause work** on the bfcache rejoin flake. Replace it with this reload and one e2e test: go back, the page reloads, and nothing is written with a stale epoch.
